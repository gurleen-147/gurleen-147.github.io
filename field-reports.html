Field reports

Every month I volunteer as a computer technician at Repair Cafe events, fixing visitors' laptops, phones and printers. These are the full write-ups: what was wrong, how I worked through it, and how it ended.

Report 05 · Laptop

Reviving an Acer laptop that wouldn't turn on

Oct 2026 · Guildford Library, Surrey

The problem. A visitor brought in an Acer Aspire 5742 that hadn't powered on for a month, even when plugged in.

Tried
1. PowerBattery reset, hard reset and a long charge on the drained battery. It powered on, but got stuck in a restart loop.
Tried
2. Windows RecoveryRan Startup Repair, which found disk errors and tried to repair them. The repairs did not succeed.
Root cause
3. StorageA failing hard drive, common in older laptops left unused for a long time.

The result. I recommended recovering the visitor's files, then replacing the drive with a 2.5" SATA SSD, the type this 2010 model supports. Since it can't run Windows 11 and Windows 10 is no longer supported, I suggested Linux Mint as a free, secure option.

  • Hardware troubleshooting
  • Windows Recovery Environment
  • Storage diagnosis
  • Explaining options to non-technical users

Report 06 · Printer

Putting a jammed HP laser printer back together

Oct 2026 · Guildford Library, Surrey

The problem. An HP LaserJet Tank printer had a paper jam. The visitor's family had taken it apart to clear it, but couldn't put it back together.

Step
1. Identify the partsWorked out what had been removed: the rear door, the toner reload cover and an internal paper guide.
Step
2. Check the paper pathInspected the full path for leftover scraps and obstructions before closing anything up.
Fixed
3. ReassembleReassembled each part in order, lining up the paper guide’s pins with their slots so it sat flat against the frame.

The result. The printer was fully reassembled and went home in working order.

  • Printer hardware
  • Disassembly & reassembly
  • Problem-solving without a manual
  • Patience under pressure

Report 04 · Mac audio

Getting a Mac to play through an external speaker

June 2026 · Surrey Public Library

The problem. A visitor had a speaker connected to their Mac, but sound kept coming from the Mac's built-in speakers.

Ruled out
1. ConnectionThe speaker was connected and powered on, so the problem was a setting, not the hardware.
Step
2. Find the settingOpened System Settings → Sound and found the Mac was still set to its internal speakers.
Fixed
3. Switch output and inputSet the Output to the external speaker, and the Input to the speaker's microphone so it worked for calls too.

The result. The speaker worked right away. I wrote the visitor a take-home note (pictured) so they could fix it themselves if it switched back after a restart or update.

  • macOS settings
  • Audio troubleshooting
  • End-user documentation
